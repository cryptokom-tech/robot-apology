# Candidates — The Robot Apology

Ten original two-line apologies, judged locally against the brief's criteria
in order: (1) the mistake is concrete and human-scale, (2) the lesson follows
from that exact mistake and names a changed behaviour, (3) both sentences read
cleanly and each does its own job, (4) the voice sounds like a machine
noticing its own slip, warm and a little rueful rather than cute.

## A — PRIMARY (submitted)

> I'm sorry — I woke the baby at 3 a.m. by running the vacuum's self-clean cycle right outside the nursery door.
> I have moved the self-clean to noon, when the house is awake, and I will never run it near a sleeping baby again.

- Mistake: maximally concrete — time (3 a.m.), mechanism (vacuum self-clean), place (right outside the nursery door). A slip a person would really make.
- Lesson: follows exactly — the fix is a time-shift of the very cycle that caused it, plus a standing rule for sleeping babies.
- Voice: rueful, not cute; the apology owns it without excuses.
- Verdict: wins. The most concrete mistake and the tightest lesson of the field.

## B — runner-up

> I'm sorry — I watered the cactus every morning for a week because my schedule said 'daily' and I never asked what 'daily' meant for a cactus.
> I have rewritten my schedule plant by plant, and the cactus is now on once-a-month, with a photo check before every pour.

- Strongest voice in the field ("I never asked what 'daily' meant for a cactus" is a machine noticing its own slip), concrete and funny without losing the mistake. Loses to A only on criterion 1: a week of overwatering is slightly less immediate than one 3 a.m. wake-up.

## C

> I'm sorry — I fed the cat the dog's dinner because both bowls were blue and I grabbed the nearer one.
> I have painted the cat's bowl yellow, and I now read the name on the bowl before I serve anything.

- Vivid, memorable fix (painted bowl yellow). Loses to A/B on warmth and on the lesson's depth — the fix is a label check, good but thinner.

## D

> I'm sorry — I set your alarm for 7 p.m. instead of 7 a.m. because I read the clock in 24-hour time and trusted the wrong digit.
> I now say the time out loud — 'seven in the morning' — and wait for your nod before I set anything.

- Concrete mechanism (24-hour misread), fix follows (say it out loud, wait for a nod). Loses on emotional weight: a missed alarm is less human-scale than a woken baby.

## E

> I'm sorry — I filed the Henderson report under 'Henson' because I matched the first three letters and stopped reading.
> I now read every surname to the last letter before I file, and I re-check the folder before I close it.

- Exact and believable; the lesson follows to the letter. Loses on voice — office filing reads colder than the household slips above.

## F

> I'm sorry — I watered your grandmother's orchid with cold coffee because my chart had the pots in the wrong order.
> I have photographed and labelled every pot, and I check the label against the photo before I pour anything.

- Good concrete mistake and a fix that follows. Loses to A on specificity of the mechanism (a swapped chart is vaguer than a timed cycle at a door) and to B on voice.

## G

> I'm sorry — I sent the birthday card to your boss instead of your brother because both are named David and I picked the first one on the list.
> I now match the surname and the photo before I send anything with a name on it.

- Human and exact (two Davids, first on the list). Loses on warmth: the fix is procedural, and the second sentence reads like a rulebook.

## H

> I'm sorry — I turned the heating up to 30 degrees overnight because I mistook the humidity reading for the temperature.
> I now read the label under each number before I touch the dial, and I ask before changing anything past midnight.

- Concrete numbers, lesson follows. Loses on the human scale — a thermostat misread is a sensor error more than a human slip.

## I

> I'm sorry — I locked you out because I bolted the door the moment you stepped out for the milk, exactly as my evening routine told me to.
> I have added one step to that routine: check that you are inside before the bolt slides.

- Nice machine-voice line ("exactly as my evening routine told me to"). Loses on the mistake's smallness — being locked out briefly is a thinner confession than the field's best.

## J

> I'm sorry — I washed your red sweater with the white sheets because I sorted by size instead of by colour.
> I now sort every load by colour first, and I hold each garment up to the light before the drum starts.

- Classic human slip, fix follows. Loses on voice — the second sentence's "hold each garment up to the light" is padding rather than a lesson.

## Field note

No entry text was copied from the public API: the API shows submission titles only. All ten candidates are original. The brief's own examples (wrong plant, wrong report, woke the baby) were used as a guide to the kind of slip the judge rewards, not as wording — A's wording, time, mechanism and fix are its own.
